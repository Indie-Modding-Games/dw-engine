# battle

The battle rules (`dwe::game::battle`, `game` layer) as a deterministic, screenless simulation: the loop
at 20 frames per second, commands, obedience, the partner's and enemies' AI, techniques, damage, blocking
and counterattack, status effects, the finisher, the end of the battle and its consequences, rewards,
technique learning, and enemies that join. The rules are in the
[reference sheet](../../reference/battle.md).

It knows nothing about the partner or saves: it does not include `packages/game/partner` or `packages/game/save`. It receives `Combatant` (declared here)
and the partner's condition, and returns a `BattleResult` that the session applies. It has no state to save: a battle
starts and ends within one session. The numbers come from the ruleset's `battle` section, and the decisions (AI,
damage, obedience) are named strategies.

## Usage

```cpp
namespace battle = dwe::game::battle;

// When loading the ruleset and the tables.
auto rules = battle::readBattleRules(store->current());                // `battle` section
auto moves = battle::MoveCatalog::build(moveTable, *rules);
auto strategies = battle::createStrategies(battleSection);             // partner_ai, enemy_ai, damage, obedience

// When a battle starts.
battle::BattleSetup setup{.partner = partnerCombatant, .condition = {...}, .enemies = {...}, ...};
setup.kind = battle::BattleKind::Arena;                                // the session reads it in the result
setup.recruitOnVictory = "resident_id";                                 // recruitment battle
auto fight = battle::Battle::start(std::move(setup), *rules, *moves, std::move(*strategies), seed);

// Every frame (or advance(seconds) with real time).
fight->command(battle::Command::Attack);                               // error if it is not in the menu
fight->pressFinisherCharge();
for (const battle::BattleEvent& event : fight->step()) { /* animate, play sound, show */ }
if (fight->finished()) {
    const battle::BattleResult& result = *fight->result();             // the session applies care, bits, items...
}

// Enemies that join after the battle, and screenless simulation.
auto joins = battle::rollJoiningEnemies(*rules, {.partnerType = ..., .inventory = ..., .scripted = false}, types, random);
battle::WinRates rates = battle::runBattles(setup, *rules, *moves, factory, {.count = 1000, .firstSeed = 1});
```

- **Positions:** all battle positions (`Point`) are in world axes, the field's (`assets::toWorldPosition`).
  The conversion from data to world happens only once, at the boundary between field and battle (`mapBattleRequest`): each
  enemy starts where its NPC is on the field (`enemyFromMap`), the tamer and the partner where they are, and what a
  defeated enemy drops lands where it ended up.
- **Tamer inputs:** `command`, `pressFinisherCharge` and the bag: `openBag`, `closeBag` and `throwItem`, which throws
  a `ThrownItem` (`items.hpp`: healing, revive, cure statuses, protection and discs) that reaches the partner after the
  frames of `battle.item_throw`. The session builds the `ThrownItem` from the effect of the inventory item
  (`BattleAdapter::throwItem`). The direct effects (`restorePartner`, `curePartnerStatus`, `protectPartner`,
  `revivePartner`) remain for tools. Their events come out on the next `step`.
- **Events:** `BattleEvent` with `EventKind`, the fighter, the other one, the technique, an amount, the status and the command.
  The events of a `step` are valid until the next call.
- **Texts:** only localization keys (`text_keys.hpp`: `battle.command.<command>`, result, statuses, item
  obtained, technique learned, injury, bits, countdown).
- **What the session does:** apply `BattleResult` (stats with their caps, care, food, lives, item loss,
  minutes, crossed hour), save, autosave according to `kind` and `recruitJoined`, and register `checkBattleSection` in
  the app's section checks.

To measure balance without writing code: `dwe-sim --scenario battle --param partner=<species> --param
enemies=<species>` ([Simulations](../../guides/simulations.md)).

## Data

- `content/packs/game/rules/original/battle.yaml`: the `battle` section with every number cited from the decomp, the
  strategies and the quirks (`quirks:`). Its schema is `content/schemas/ruleset_battle.schema.json`.
- The technique table (`assets::MoveTable`, from the user's disc) gives power, MP, range, distance,
  specialty, status and learning probabilities; no species, technique or item table goes into git.
- The battle clock is its own: frame 1 at the start, 20 per second (`FramesPerSecond`); the game clock is
  stopped and then advances `elapsedMinutes`.

### Design lead decisions (2026-10-20)

- **No MP on either side:** faithful to the original, the battle has no time limit. Only the simulations and the
  tests set a frame cap.
- **`TYPE_FACTORS`, `FLEE_CHANCES` and the gain factors:** they stay as ruleset constants with their decomp
  citation; they are not extracted from the disc.
- **Recruits:** the session decides with `recruitOnVictory` and reads `recruitJoined`; the battle does not look at the map.
- **Per-hour care counters during the battle:** the session or the partner applies them with `elapsedMinutes`,
  `hourCrossed` and `lifetimeHoursLost`; the battle stays independent.
- **Careful off-screen enemy:** the field tells the battle where each fighter is drawn
  (`BattleGround::fighterNodeOnScreen`); done on 2026-11-01.

### Design lead decisions (2026-10-21)

- **How a battle starts:** the original only starts them from the NPC scripts (`script_interp.c`: the
  `START_BATTLE` operation); there are no random encounters. The map scripts already run and start them that way
  (`start_battle`); there is also the debug trigger `battle("")` of the console and of the autotests
  (`apps/game/src/battle_trigger.cpp`).
- **Enemy name on its HP panel:** the original does not write it; its HP bars sit next to the enemies in the 3D
  scene (`battle_main.c: BTL_renderEnemyHPBars`). The original look already draws them that way (2026-10-30); the modern
  one still shows the panel with the name.
- **"Has escapado." (`battle.outcome.fled`):** the original gives the final box no title
  (`battle_ui.c: handleBattleEndBox`). The screen says how the battle ended (victory or defeat) as a documented
  accessibility deviation, and it stays. A flee has no final box (2026-11-03, #179), so
  that title no longer shows.
- **The bag in battle is the field's (QA-M19, 2026-10-30):** the 2026-10-21 deviation (its own box with the
  title "Throw an item", dashes in the empty slots and throwing on confirming the slot) is withdrawn; the battle opens the field
  bag with its options box, like the original. `battle_throw` stays for the console and the tests.
- **Throw frame 7 (measured, 2026-11-01):** the item leaves on frame 7 of the player's animation,
  which the item routine sees on frame 6 after throwing it; it lands 29 frames after throwing it (before: 31).

### PC adaptations (2026-10-28)

- **Finisher prompt:** with the bar full the original writes "FINISH!!" and, in a gray box, the pad's square button
  and "PUSH" (a single `BI_IN` sprite). On PC the square says nothing, so the original look crops
  the sprite to "PUSH" and puts the interface prompt in its place (`ui.prompt("extra")`, the theme's `key` class) with
  the `extra` key (Q by default) or the button of the pad in use (the west one: Square, X…), according to the last
  device and the controls the player has assigned. With a prompt of one or two letters (Q, X, Y…) the box stays
  where the original puts it, to the right of "FINISH!!"; with a longer one (the pad's Square) it drops a row, below
  "FINISH!!", with the same right edge, and the finisher button for the mouse (`.command.no-icon`) drops with it.
  With the mouse the finisher still responds to a click on the box.

## Effect scripts

`EffectRunner` (`effect_runner.hpp`) runs the moves' effect scripts (`assets::EffectScript`, read from
`effect/<move>/script.yaml`) like the original: `load` per move of each fighter (`BTL_loadMoveEFE`), `start` runs the
start script (`BTL_startEFE`) and returns the first instance it launched, `tick` is one battle frame
(`BTL_tickEFEEngine`: adds the frame of each active sub-effect and runs its tick script) and `stop` cuts an effect
(`BTL_removeMoveEffect`). Each slot has its own copy of the data, the stack, the calls, `spawn` and the sub-effects
follow the original, so each sound plays on the frame it plays on in the original.

Sounds go out through `EffectSounds`: `play_sound` calls `play` (bank 8) and the ids 0x12 and 0x20–0x22 call `playHeld`,
which keeps the voice; the voice stops when its sub-effect ends, like `EFE_SOUND_DATA`. The hit sub-operations
(`check_collision`, `apply_*_hit`, `check_target_collision`, `is_target_unhit`) answer with the simulation's hit
(`noteHit`) instead of the original's collision boxes. `hit_target` (`BTL_addAttackObjectToTarget`) marks the
target as hit, which is what `is_target_unhit` looks at (the original's `isHit`), and the damage stays in the
simulation; Assumption: the mark lasts until the next start of the effect. Without it, the target loop of Poison
Powder repeated its sound endlessly. `random_in_range` is `customRandom`. Documented improvement:
`stopAll` stops all the kept voices, while `BTL_stopEFESounds` only looks at the first (it does not advance the pointer).

### Drawing

The other sub-operations (`effect_runner_subops.cpp`) do the original's calculations in its axes (y downward) and
units (4096 is 1; angles of 4096 per turn, 512 in `_sin`/`_cos`), with the libgte matrices (`RotMatrix`,
`RotMatrixYXZ`, `RotMatrixZYX`) and `matrixToEuler1`/`2` in `effect_math.cpp`. The fighters are read through the
`EffectBodies` port: position, turn (`rotation.vy`), radius and height from the species table, the original type
(`battle.effects.original_types`, the table of original types of `effect_common.c`) and the matrix of each node of the
model the app draws. `start` receives attacker and target (the battle's, as in the original); the
children inherit the parent's. After `tick`, `render` runs the render scripts (`BTL_renderEFEEngine`) and leaves in
`draws()` what they drew: `EffectSprite` (`BTL_renderProjectedSprite`, `BTL_renderCenteredSprite`: position, color,
scale, texels, pivot, turn, blend and palette row) and `EffectModel` (`BTL_drawTMD`, `BTL_drawTMDYXZ`: model
object, matrix with scale, color from `set_model_object_color` and UV animation offset). The battle knows nothing
about renderers.

The session (`BattleEffects`) passes the fighters and exposes `draws()`; the app (`BattleEffectBodies`, `FieldScene::setEffects`)
puts each sprite as a billboard with the texture `effect/<move>/texture` and its palette (the colors with STP are blended with
the sprite's blend; size in the world = texels × scale / 32, which is what the original's projection gives with
`VIEWPORT_DISTANCE` 500) and each object of the model `effect/<move>/model` as its bone with the effect's matrix (the
other bones at zero), tinted with its color.
